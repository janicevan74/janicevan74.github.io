Printables how-to

From PDF to finished piece

Printable wallpaper, floors, boxes and labels are some of the easiest ways to fill a room. These steps keep them the right size and looking like the real thing.

  1. Print at 100%

    Choose “Actual size” or 100% in the print window, and turn off “Fit to page.”

    If the printable has a test square or ruler, measure it before printing the rest. If it's off, every piece will be off.

  2. Match the paper to the piece

    Thin paper wraps and rolls. Heavy paper holds a fold. See the paper guide below.

    Set the paper type in your printer settings too, so the printer puts down the right amount of ink.

  3. Let the ink dry

    Give inkjet prints at least 15 minutes, longer on photo paper, before cutting or gluing.

    A light coat of matte spray sealer keeps ink from smearing when glue touches it. Spray outdoors.

  4. Cut on a mat with a fresh blade

    Use a craft knife, a metal ruler and a self-healing mat for straight cuts; small scissors for curves.

    Change the blade often. A dull blade drags and tears paper instead of cutting it.

  5. Score before you fold

    Run an empty ballpoint pen or a bone folder along a ruler on each fold line, then fold.

    Scored folds are crisp and don't crack, which matters most on cartons and book covers.

  6. Color the white edges

    Cut paper shows a white edge. Run a marker or colored pencil in a matching color along it.

    This one step makes boxes, books and signs look solid instead of paper-thin.

  7. Glue thin and smooth

    Use a glue stick or spray adhesive for flat pieces like wallpaper and floors. Use tacky glue for tabs and 3D pieces, spread thin with a toothpick.

    Too much wet glue makes paper ripple. Smooth from the center out with an old card.

How it's made

From printed sheet to a shelf of groceries

A batch of 1:12 grocery packaging on the workbench. The cutting mat's grid is in inches, so you can see how small each piece is.

Tip: cans from a dowel

Wrap printed can labels around a wooden dowel, leaving a small gap between them, and glue each seam. Once dry, cut the dowel between the labels and you have a row of matching cans. A ¼ in dowel makes a can about 3 in across at 1:12.

Piece doesn't fit?

  • Check the print scale. Most size problems come from printing at less than 100%.
  • Check the printable's scale matches your room, 1:12 or 1:24.
  • Measure your box inside, not outside. Walls take up space.
