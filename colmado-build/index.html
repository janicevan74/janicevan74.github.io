Build diary

Colmado Doña Carmen

A 1:12 Puerto Rican corner store, built as a gift for my mom, Carmen. Every box, can and bottle on these shelves is printed paper. Newest steps are at the top; tap any photo to see it bigger.

Christmas on the storefront

The store sign went on the header, the strip across the top of the open front. Under it hangs a little ¡Feliz Navidad! banner: each pennant is cut out, folded over a thread and glued in place on a printed spacing guide, so the letters come out evenly spaced. A poinsettia garland finishes the edge.

A paper bag for the counter

A brown paper grocery bag stamped with the store's name, folded from one printed piece with a zigzag top, like the bags the colmado used to pack your groceries in.

Stocking the shelves

Bottles and coffee went up first, then the cans, then the boxes and bags, working from the top shelf down so my hands didn't knock anything over.

Shortcuts for the tiniest items

Folding dozens of bottles one by one gets tiring fast. Instead, a whole row of bottles is printed on one strip that stands on the shelf with a small foam-board block behind it. From the front it reads as a full row. The cans are rolled one at a time.

Printing, cutting and folding the groceries

Every box starts as a flat printed net: Brisa detergent, Isla Dorada crackers and sugar, coffee, cornstarch and Doña Carmen rice. Cut, score the folds, then glue. The cutting mat's grid is in inches, so you can see how small each one is.

The shell

Weathered two-tone walls, green above and deep red below, a patterned cement-tile floor and a pair of worn blue louvered doors. I papered the back wall first, then the side walls.
