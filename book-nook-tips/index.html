Book nook tips for beginners

Five things I've learned building book nook kits, the little worlds that slide between the books on a shelf. I wish someone had told me these before my first one.

1 Glue it, even if the instructions don't say to

Some kits only tell you to slot pieces together. Use your judgment: if a piece feels loose or could fall out when you move the book nook, put a small dab of glue on it. Pieces that only press in tend to loosen over time.

2 Use a quilling tool for scrolls

When a kit has you roll paper scrolls, like rolled maps, documents or decorations, a quilling tool makes it much easier. Slip the end of the strip into the slot and turn. The rolls come out tight and even, and your fingers stay out of the glue.

3 Keep it organized with a file organizer

Book nook kits come with lots of sheets and tiny parts. A file organizer keeps the sheets sorted so you can find the right piece when a step calls for it, and small parts don't get lost.

4 Go easy on thin wood

Some kits use much thinner wood than others. Pressing too hard, whether you're popping a piece out of its sheet or fitting it in place, can snap it. Push gently, and if a piece won't come out, cut the little tabs holding it with a craft knife instead of forcing it.

5 Sand it instead of forcing it

If a piece is too tight to fit, don't force it. Sand the edge a little with fine sandpaper or a sanding stick, test the fit, and repeat until it slides in. It's slower, but forcing a tight piece is how parts crack.
