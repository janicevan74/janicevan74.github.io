Room box building

Build the room in the right order

A room box is a single room in a box with the front open, like a little stage. Working in this order means nothing gets in the way of the next step.

First, measure the inside

Sellers usually list a box's outside size. The inside is smaller by the thickness of the walls, often ¼ to ½ in. Measure inside width, depth and height before you cut any paper, and write them down.

  1. Plan the lighting

    Decide where lights go before anything is covered. Drill holes and run wires or LED strips now.

    Hide battery packs behind the back wall or under a raised floor.

  2. Do the ceiling

    Paint or paper the ceiling while nothing is in the way.

    Small gaps at the edges are fine. Crown molding will cover them.

  3. Cover the walls

    Two ways work. Run one continuous strip from the left wall, across the back, to the right wall, pressing it into the corners. Or paper the back wall first, letting about ¼ in wrap onto each side wall, then lay the side pieces over it so their edges sit right in the corners.

    The strip leaves no seams in the corners. Back-first is easier to handle, since every piece is short, and the corner seams barely show. Either way, leave a little extra at the top and bottom and trim it after.

  4. Lay the floor

    Printed flooring, scrapbook paper or coffee stirrers for wood planks all work.

    Run planks front to back. It makes a shallow box look deeper.

  5. Add the trim

    Baseboards, chair rail and crown molding hide the gaps where walls, floor and ceiling meet.

    Trim is what makes a box start to look like a real room.

  6. Furnish, then layer

    Place the largest pieces first, then fill in with small things.

    Glue down only once you're happy with the layout.

    Making it feel lived-in

    • Use warm light. Warm white LEDs (around 2700–3000K) feel like a home. Cool white looks like an office.
    • Leave a little mess. A cup on the table, an open book, a towel over a chair. Perfect rooms look like showrooms.
    • Group small things in odd numbers and at different heights. Three jars look arranged; two look lined up.
    • Age it gently. A light dry-brush of brown or gray on edges and corners takes away the brand-new look.
