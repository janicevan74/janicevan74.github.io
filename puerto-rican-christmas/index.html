Seasonal guide

A Puerto Rican Christmas in miniature

A Puerto Rican Christmas isn't one day. It's two months of parrandas, pasteles, coquito and the Three Kings, and every one of those traditions can go into a room box. Here's what to include, how big each piece should be, and how to make them in paper.

The season, in order

  • Late November: The lights go up and the first parrandas start: friends showing up at your door at night with a cuatro, a güiro and a pandero, singing until you let them in.
  • December 24 · Nochebuena: The big family dinner: lechón, arroz con gandules, pasteles, and coquito to toast.
  • December 31 · Despedida de año: Seeing the old year out with family, food and music.
  • January 5–6 · Día de Reyes: The night before, children fill a shoebox with grass for the Three Kings' camels and leave it under the bed. In the morning, the grass is gone and there are gifts.
  • After Reyes · Octavitas: Eight more days of celebrating after Three Kings Day.
  • Mid-January · SanSe: The Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastián in Old San Juan close the season. That's why people say Puerto Rico has the longest Christmas in the world.

How to make them in paper

Pasteles

Cut tiny rectangles of green paper, or print a banana-leaf pattern, and fold each around a small block of foam board. Tie it with one strand of embroidery thread in a cross. Stack them on a tray made of silver cardstock.

Coquito

A wine bottle is about 3 in across, so ¼ in at 1:12. Roll a cream label around a ¼ in dowel like the cans in the paper groceries tutorial, then cut it to 1 in tall. For a whole shelf of bottles, use one printed strip.

Flor de Pascua

Cut small red leaf shapes, layer two or three rounds of them, and add a dot of yellow paint in the center. Set them on green leaves in a paper pot wrapped with a strip of foil paper.

Wreaths and garland

A ring of green paper or yarn on a cardstock circle, with a bow cut from red paper. Hang one on each door. A printed poinsettia garland along the top edge of a room box frames the whole scene.

The grass box for the Reyes

Fold a small open box from brown cardstock, fill it with green shredded paper, and add a label: para los camellos. It's the detail every Puerto Rican recognizes right away.

Parranda instruments

A printed cuatro glued to cardstock looks great leaned against a wall or a counter. Make a güiro from a rolled paper tube with fine lines drawn across it, and a pandero from a thin slice of a cardboard tube with paper stretched over one side.

Light it warm

A string of warm white battery fairy lights across the top of the room makes any Christmas scene glow. Tuck the battery pack behind the roof or the back wall. See the scale ruler to check sizes, and the glue guide for what to use on each material.
